# Five-minute pitch — working script and capture plan

Target: about five minutes at a measured pace, including short gameplay pauses. Rehearse and time the recording; this is not a completed video or a video URL. Bergen should rewrite this in her voice and replace provisional statements with findings from the three actual playtests.

**0:00–0:35 · Title and promise**

Halcyon Academy is a narrative game about learning to understand yourself inside a city built to keep you safe. You play Molly Duvernay during her first days at an academy founded by her father. The city is comfortable and beautiful. Her father loves her. Neither fact makes belonging simple. First Weather is a playable two-day slice of that larger game, set in a fictional flooded New Orleans beneath the Cloche.

[Show the title, then the district. Let the artwork breathe for five seconds.]

**0:35–1:25 · How it plays**

The player moves between a residence, classrooms, canal steps and a greenhouse. Each afternoon offers limited time: prepare the assignment, rest, or spend time with someone. Choices carry into the following morning. The barometer is central, but I want it to become something players read through experience. It affects the rhythm of Molly’s thoughts and the sound around her. Morning routines offer different kinds of help and difficulty. This prototype demonstrates those tradeoffs without asking the player to master a long tutorial.

[Show one morning choice, a short dialogue response, and the activity map.]

**1:25–2:15 · Why the systems matter**

A high-pressure classroom episode changes Molly’s remaining timetable. Studying changes what she can bring to the class even when the morning is difficult. The evening offers another kind of choice: call home, message Jules, write privately, or sleep. Alaric’s call is warm, yet the needle can rise during it. That is the sort of tension the game explores: help can be real without being complete. The prototype also gives helpful medicine an observable role rather than treating it as a moral failure.

[Contrast brief prepared/unprepared class captures; show the call and journal.]

**2:15–3:05 · Lola and the garden**

The greenhouse introduces Lola Landry. She is building something for home, helping care for her grandmother, and managing her own place at the academy. Her Rootwork becomes part of play when she feels what the beds need. There are six measures of water and more need than supply. Molly’s mentor has shaped what she protects first. The player chooses a shortage, then responds when the shade changes. The relationship develops through that work: questions, friction, attention and the promise to return.

[Show allocating water and Lola’s clue; hold the final dispatch briefly without reading every line aloud.]

**3:05–4:00 · Aesthetic direction and revisions**

The target is an illustrated world that feels inhabited. The new title established chunky painted color, indigo shadows, warm gold and New Orleans ironwork. The interface now uses those materials and serif typography. The academy has painted moving students, a live clock, changing light and water. The map can be explored with a trackpad, with buttons and keyboard alternatives. This remains layered two-dimensional art; the goal is intimacy and activity, not a claim that the prototype is a finished three-dimensional city.

The first feedback themes were that the world was interesting but not immersive enough, and that the reason to keep playing was unclear. This revision addresses those themes through visual coherence and consequences, ending with a personal invitation rather than a scorecard.

**4:00–5:00 · Current state and next gate**

The current prototype is built in Unity. It has a title, new game and continuation, two playable days, multiple morning and activity routes, classroom consequences, an evening, a saved journal, a garden decision and clear completion. It also includes credits, notices, larger text, reduced motion and pause controls. Automated checks cover routes and presentation, while three documented human playtests are still needed before final submission. The dialogue is an editable working draft that I will author myself.

The next gate is evidence that players want to return to this world and these people. The full quarter, wider Ward travel and later revelations are beyond this slice. I want this first chapter to earn that larger ambition through a smaller experience: a player learning how Molly hears the world, and finding someone she wants to hear it with.
